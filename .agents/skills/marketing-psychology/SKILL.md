# Marketing psychology (apply, don't decorate)

Output: principles are applied inside the copy, brief or audit that called this skill (English files); explanations in chat are in the chat language from `config/operator.md`.

Use at most two or three principles per asset. Every application must be true: real dates, real sources, the product's own promises (`config/product.md`), the audience's real pains (`config/audiences.md`), claims allowed by `config/claims.md`.

| Principle | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Jobs to be done | Name the job the audience hires the product for ("know what is true before I commit"), not the activity ("browse"). Lead with the job. |
| Loss aversion | The cost or surprise found out after committing beats "save money". Words, not unverified numbers. |
| Contrast effect | A catalogue of everything vs what fits your wish; a seller's brochure vs figures with sources and dates. |
| Availability | One vivid, true moment (a real screen recording with private data blurred) beats a list of features. |
| Authority | Sources with dates, the professional services the page names. Not "experts say". |
| Social proof | Only numbers and testimonials that exist and that the company confirms. None pre-launch; never invented. |
| Risk reversal | Saying the product's limits plainly is honesty, which reads as safety. "No cost to you" lines wait for clearance in `config/claims.md`. |
| Anchoring | A real date as the anchor for action ("opens <date>"); never a price or fee anchor the page does not state. |
| Hyperbolic discounting | A near, concrete benefit ("be on the list the day it opens") over a distant one ("decide better over the years"). |
| Identity / unity | The address form and warmth `config/brand.md` prescribes, the audience's own moment ("your new chapter"). Never religion, origin or another personal attribute. |
| Paradox of choice | One CTA per screen, the funnel's own ask. |
| Goal gradient | The product's steps from the first wish to the outcome as a visible path. |
| Pratfall | Admit the limits (what the product does not do; that it is not launched yet, when true) to raise trust. Never a word on the forbidden list. |
| Curse of knowledge | Test copy on a real member of the audience who has not seen the page; local jargon and acronyms need a one-line explanation for audiences outside the primary market. |

**Banned:** fake scarcity, invented testimonials, yield or return promises, pressure language, fear-mongering about suppliers or competitors, any figure without a source and date.
